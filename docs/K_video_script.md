# HeatShield proposal video: 2-minute script and storyboard

**Use:** GSC 2026–27 Phase 1 Ignite project overview video. The official
[program guide](https://www.globalsustainabilitychallenge.org/challenge-details/)
lists a **2-min project overview video** as a deliverable and currently lists
an application deadline of **24 November 2026, 11:59 AM UTC**. The requested
**22 November** date is treated here as the team's internal deadline. The
public guide does not state aspect ratio, file size, or upload encoding; check
the application portal before export.

**Important story boundary:** HeatShield has no worker interview available
for this script, and no interview quote is used. The opening is a clearly
labelled illustrative scenario, not a real participant or a claim from an
interview. Do not substitute or add an identifiable worker's image, voice,
workplace, or quote without specific informed permission for public video use.

**Demo boundary:** the current backend describes its responses as synthetic
mock data. Label the screen recording “prototype / illustrative data”; do not
present its values, resource pins, or risk scores as live field results.
See [K_backend.md](./K_backend.md) and [R_systems_map.md](./R_systems_map.md).

## Shot-by-shot script

Target approximately **215 spoken words** at a calm 110–120 words per minute,
with short visual pauses. Rehearse with a stopwatch and trim pauses rather
than speeding up. Total runtime is 2:00.

| Time | Picture / storyboard | Voice-over / dialogue | On-screen text and sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:15** | Illustrative reenactment: anonymous hands checking a delivery route in bright afternoon light; cut to a worker choosing between continuing, finding shade, or taking water. Use a staged close-up, no identifiable person, logo, or real workplace. | “Picture an outdoor worker in Rohini on a hot afternoon. The route is still unfinished. Is there shade nearby? Can they pause? A city warning matters—but it may not answer those immediate, practical questions.” | Persistent small caption: **Illustrative scenario—not an interview**. Sound: ordinary street ambience, low under narration; no alarm effect. |
| **0:15–0:32** | Draw one simple loop from [R's systems map](./R_systems_map.md): heat + sun + exertion + time → heat strain → slower work / pressure to continue → longer exposure. Show shade, water, rest, and a usable warning as interruption points. | “Heat exposure is not temperature alone. Sun, humidity, work intensity, time, and access to recovery combine. The feedback from strain to longer exposure is a local hypothesis to test, not the same story for every worker.” | Labels: **Hazard + exposure + ability to recover**; **Local feedbacks are hypotheses**. |
| **0:32–0:47** | Side-by-side: public heat warning / city plan on one side; an individual route and unverified resource list on the other. Highlight the handoff, not a false claim that existing services are ineffective. | “Official forecasts and city heat plans are essential. Our proposed gap is the handoff: helping a person interpret conditions for a planned activity, while helping a partner review where verified support may be missing.” | Caption: **Complement official warnings and heat plans**. |
| **0:47–1:16** | Screen recording of the HeatShield prototype: risk card → factors/actions → map/resources → optimizer budget → simulation. Use only a clearly marked demo dataset. Show source/time labels as overlays if not present in the screen capture. | “HeatShield brings those steps into one prototype. A risk card explains the estimate and its main factors. A map can show heat hotspots and checked resources. A city partner can compare candidate interventions under a budget. Today’s API screen uses synthetic demo data; connecting validated forecasts and field observations is still pilot work.” | Persistent watermark: **Prototype • synthetic demo values**. Avoid showing participant data, exact user locations, credentials, or personal account details. |
| **1:16–1:42** | Three measurable cards: comprehension, sensor QA, budget-plan audit. Show “baseline first” and sample-size markers; no fabricated result bars. | “We will test before claiming impact. With 20 to 50 consenting adults across delivery, construction, street-vending, and campus work, we will measure whether people understand the risk card and can name a feasible action. We will report sensor data completeness and comparison error. For city planning, we will check that every scenario stays within budget and compare its modelled coverage with a simple baseline. These are pilot measures—not proof of fewer illnesses or income saved.” | Labels: **Comprehension • data quality • budget feasibility**; **Baseline first; results not yet collected**. |
| **1:42–2:00** | Team appears or names/roles on neutral background. End card: “Help us test safely in DTU/Rohini.” | “Our ask: help us secure a permitted pilot site, a local heat-health reviewer, and trusted channels to invite adult participants. HeatShield cannot create shade, water, paid breaks, or safer work by itself. With partners, we can test whether better evidence helps people act and cities target support.” | End card: **HeatShield | DTU/Rohini pilot**; **Seeking site permission, expert review, and partner feedback**. Do not imply any named institution has endorsed the project. |

## Edit and submission checklist

- [ ] Final export is no longer than 2:00; confirm the portal accepts the chosen
  aspect ratio, codec, file size, and upload method.
- [ ] Keep the opening labelled as an illustrative scenario; no fabricated
  interview, quotation, participant, or outcome.
- [ ] Every interface value and map pin shown is labelled mock/illustrative
  unless it has been checked and documented as real.
- [ ] Do not show personal data, raw interviews, consent forms, survey
  responses, credentials, or private partner communications.
- [ ] Use only original/appropriately licensed footage, images, music, and
  map attribution. Obtain public-use permission for any identifiable person.
- [ ] Add captions; check intelligibility on a phone speaker and avoid flashing
  heat-warning graphics that could be mistaken for an official alert.
- [ ] Team watches the complete export, checks the runtime, and uploads through
  the authorized GSC portal. This script does not itself produce or submit a
  video.
