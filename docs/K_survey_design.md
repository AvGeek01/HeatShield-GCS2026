# HeatShield student heat-experience survey design

## GSC fit and submission constraints

Checked 10 November 2026 against the [Maker Bhavan Foundation's official Global Sustainability Challenge page](https://makerbhavanfoundation.org/gsc/). Its published Phase 1 – Ignite description asks teams to form, choose a theme, research problems and engage stakeholders, then submit **team registration, a problem statement, a solution concept and an early mockup**. The official page describes Adaptation and Resilience broadly, including resilience of people, infrastructure and natural systems, and other critical adaptation needs. Heat resilience is a reasonable problem area within that theme; the page does not list “Heat Resilience” as a separate formal track.

The official page does **not** publish an Ignite application template, required file format, word/character limits, or a Heat Resilience-specific rule set. It gives a 2025 Ignite schedule and a 3 November 2025 deadline; this is not evidence of the 2026 cycle's current rules. Confirm the current application portal and any team-specific submission guide before preparing an application. Do not treat this survey's format or length as a GSC submission requirement.

The survey is stakeholder research for the Ignite problem statement and concept; it is not a substitute for the GSC application or an official GSC deliverable format.

## Purpose and audience

Understand college students' reported heat exposure, current coping, and preferences for HeatShield's personal heat guidance and campus hotspot/intervention planning. This is exploratory student research, not a medical assessment or a representative city survey.

### Recruitment segments and response targets

Recruit primarily around DTU and the Rohini-area pilot. Students may fit more than one segment, so the ranges below are **overlapping recruitment targets**, not mutually exclusive quotas. The survey captures segment membership with a select-all-that-apply question; tag every selected segment. Target at least 100 valid responses, including at least 60 from DTU, before interpreting or reporting group-level results.

| Segment | Target | Recruitment emphasis |
|---|---:|---|
| DTU day scholars who commute | 50–60 | Class groups, class representatives, and campus QR locations |
| DTU hostel residents | 30–40 | Hostel groups and hostel common areas, with permission |
| Students in outdoor-heavy activities (sports, NCC/NSS, fieldwork, workshops, fest volunteers) | 20–30 | Relevant clubs, teams, activity coordinators |
| Students with outdoor part-time work (gig delivery, event staff, commuting tutors) | 10–20 | Student groups and peer referrals; do not recruit or describe this as a worker sample |
| Other Delhi-NCR college students (comparison group) | About 30 | Peer networks and college groups outside DTU |

These goals sum to more than 100 because people can count in multiple DTU segments. “At least 60 DTU” is a separate minimum. Aim for the stated segment ranges where feasible, but do not claim a quota-balanced or representative sample. Ask respondents to select each segment that applies.

## Form opening and eligibility

### Start-page copy (English)

**HeatShield: Student Heat Experiences in Delhi**

We are a student team taking part in the Global Sustainability Challenge. We are learning how college students experience hot weather and what support could make campus travel and daily activities safer and easier.

Taking part is voluntary and takes about 4 minutes. The survey is intended for college students aged 18 or older. Answers are anonymous: we do not ask for your name, roll number, phone number, or email address in this survey. Please do not include identifying details in the place-name answer. You may stop at any time by closing the form; unanswered forms are not used as completed responses.

This survey is for research and product design only. It is not medical advice, and it does not diagnose or treat any condition. A separate, optional pilot sign-up form appears after the survey; it is not connected to your answers.

1. **Required age gate**
   - English: “Are you 18 years old or older?”
   - हिन्दी: “क्या आपकी आयु 18 वर्ष या उससे अधिक है?”
   - “Yes / हाँ” routes to the required consent checkbox and then the research questions. “No / नहीं” routes to a polite end page: “Thank you for your interest. This survey is only for people aged 18 or older. It ends here; no research questions will be shown. / आपकी रुचि के लिए धन्यवाद। यह सर्वेक्षण केवल 18 वर्ष या उससे अधिक आयु के लोगों के लिए है। यह यहीं समाप्त होता है; शोध संबंधी प्रश्न नहीं दिखाए जाएँगे।”
   - Google Forms may save the gate-only response when the “No” path submits. It must not be counted as an eligible research response.
2. **Required consent checkbox** (shown only after the respondent passes the age gate)
   - English: “I have read the information above and voluntarily agree to take this anonymous survey.”
   - हिन्दी: “मैंने ऊपर दी गई जानकारी पढ़ ली है और इस गुमनाम सर्वेक्षण में स्वेच्छा से भाग लेने के लिए सहमत हूँ।”
   - One checkbox, required. If a person does not agree, they cannot continue; the opening page tells them they may close the form.

## Distribution plan

1. **Check permission first.** Ask the relevant DTU office/department, faculty contact, hostel administration and student-club leads whether student surveys, QR posters, or group messages require approval. Follow any required ethics, faculty, or campus process; do not imply DTU endorsement.
2. **Pilot before launch.** Complete the five-student check in [K_survey_pilot_checklist.md](./K_survey_pilot_checklist.md), fix issues, and only then share the live survey.
3. **Avoid exam weeks.** Confirm the academic calendar with class representatives. Do not launch or repeatedly message during exams; adjust dates if an exam or campus approval delay overlaps the collection window.

## Quality controls and data handling

- The opening and distribution copy asks each person to answer once. Google Forms cannot enforce one response per person without sign-in; **do not require sign-in** because anonymity and access take priority.
- The under-18 branch ends before the research section. Google Forms may still save a gate-only response when its “No” branch submits; exclude those rows from eligible-response counts and do not treat them as survey research answers.
- Before analysis, screen for obvious duplicate submissions and test entries (for example, identical response patterns with implausibly short completion times, or known team test timestamps). Do not claim that duplicate screening proves unique people. Document exclusions and retain no identifying inference.
- Tag all selected segments from Question 1. Keep “other Delhi-NCR college” distinct from DTU. Record counts by segment; note that overlapping segment counts must not be added as if they were unique respondents.
- Exclude the five pilot entries and any known test entries from the analysis. If the production form is used for testing, remove test rows from the working analysis copy, not by sharing or publishing row-level data.
- In Google Forms, turn off email collection and sign-in restrictions. Send responses only to a team-owned Google Sheet shared privately with the three team members; do not enable link sharing. Confirm Drive and form access before launch. Keep the optional sign-up responses in a different form/sheet and do not join them to survey answers.
- Do not collect GPS coordinates or exact personal travel traces. The open place-name answer is optional and must be reviewed for accidentally entered identifying details before any public quotation or publication.
- Store only aggregate results in project materials. Keep any row-level responses in the private, access-restricted team workspace; do not commit them to this public repository.

## Limitations and reporting language

This is a convenience sample recruited through student networks and campus locations, not a probability sample. It is student-only, excludes under-18 students, and will have smartphone/access and self-selection bias. Heat symptoms, exposure, resource access and impacts are self-reported and subject to recall bias; the April–June 2026 recall is being asked in October–November 2026. A small or uneven sample may not support reliable comparisons between segments. Student reports do not represent outdoor workers, all Delhi residents, or all students.

Use bounded language such as **“among students surveyed,” “in this convenience sample,”** or **“respondents at DTU reported…”**. Never generalize the findings to “people,” “workers,” “Delhi residents,” or all students. Do not present reported symptoms as diagnosed illness, a medical prevalence estimate, a measured hotspot, or evidence that a specific intervention caused an outcome.
